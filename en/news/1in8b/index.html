In the middle of the AI backlash, a game called 1 in 8 Billion just blew up

The two words indie developers hide the most, browser game and AI game, and what one hit proved about both at once

October 16, 2026 · Oreum Games

The backlash against AI is at its loudest right now. Illustrators, writers and musicians are calling for people to reject AI-made work, and in games, the moment a developer admits to using AI the review page goes cold. Right in the middle of that, a game called 1 in 8 Billion blew up.

The two words developers don't want to say

There are two words indie developers, and the ones who have been at it longest most of all, would rather not say out loud. One is browser game. The other is AI game.

Browser game carries an old shadow. No install, no purchase, so it must be light, and light quietly turns into thrown together. The same game gets rated a notch lower the moment people hear it runs in a browser. That still happens today.

AI game is worse. Say you made it with AI and people assume it is a half-finished thing you barely managed to squeeze out of your head, a copy anyone could have made. That look even has a name now: AI slop. For someone who put real work in, there is no word that stings more. So plenty of developers use AI and pretend they didn't, and never write the word anywhere near their store page.

The reality is different

It is hard to find a working game developer today who uses no AI at all. Writing code, hunting bugs, generating placeholder art, running translations: it is in there somewhere. The bigger the studio, the more aggressively they use it. When companies with hundreds of staff bring in AI to cut their pipeline, there is no reason a solo developer should refuse it.

And yet the hostility hasn't cooled. Among players abroad, in the US especially, the two letters AI often end the conversation before it starts. The rating is decided before the game is played, and a post on a community goes down regardless of what it says. The tool gets judged instead of the result, and that mood has been the same for years.

It wore both words and succeeded anyway

1 in 8 Billion came out wearing both words. It is a browser game, and it is an AI game. It didn't hide either. And it succeeded.

What that says is simple. What people reject is not AI itself. It is work without quality. When the content is good, the tool it was made with stops being the question. Other fields got there first. In China an AI-made drama was put into prime time and did well. At first the phrase AI drama was itself a punchline, but once something worth watching arrived, viewers stopped asking how it was made. 1 in 8 Billion showed that games are on the same road.

Vague hostility can't be argued away. It only breaks when one good result shows up. That one has now shown up.

Sharing on social media is what made it

If you had to name one reason it succeeded, it is social media. 1 in 8 Billion did not spread through ads. People who played it posted their own screens, people who saw those posts played it and posted theirs, and it travelled from there. Because the life you live inside the game is different for everyone, every post told a different story, and each one pulled the next person in.

The makers left that road open from the start. The official site says anyone may stream or make videos of the game and the lives lived in it without asking first, and that monetized channels and TV and radio broadcasts are all fine. The only request is a credit line.

Usually a game launches with a long copyright notice attached. Ask permission first, monetized channels please enquire separately, no broadcast. 1 in 8 Billion went the other way. It let people film, post and spread it however they liked, and every one of those posts became an ad. A game has to be good to spread, but block the road and even a good game stops right there.

This is not someone else's story for Oreum Games

For Oreum Games, which makes games with AI and runs a site full of them, this is not someone else's news. Every game Oreum Games releases is a browser game, and every one is made with AI.

That is why this success is so welcome. Someone went first and showed, loudly, that an AI game people would normally push away can still reach them if the content is good.

Oreum Games has tried to make games anyone can play with nothing to install, and at the same time to find subjects nobody else has touched. Line 1 Villains, where you defend the cars of a subway train. Gosiwon Manager, where you run a tiny boarding house and study at the same time. Dev Chicken Shop, where you fry the chicken yourself. Lasso Sister, where a Black woman in the Old West rides out and ropes wanted outlaws of every background. A big company would have cut every one of these at the planning stage. They exist because a very small team had AI in its hands.

There is no real hit yet. But the number of people visiting keeps growing, and total play time passed 1,300 hours. Nobody sent them. They were searching for a quick browser game, tried one, and came back. The return rate is about 10%, which is on the high side.

Congratulations to 1 in 8 Billion. Oreum Games goes back to making the next game.
